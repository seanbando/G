# Niche Analysis — Finance Explainer Scripts (for Whiteboard Animation)

Source scripts, in the creator's preference order:

1. **Paper Billions** — "Why the stock market crashed" (Nvidia / midterms) and "The Nvidia spy" (Jensen Huang / Megaspeed)
2. **Stick Finance** — "What are stocks?"
3. **Martik Finance** — "Why the rich borrow instead of selling"

Creator note on Paper Billions: *"I love how other people / character voices come into the video when needed — makes it interesting."*

---

## 1. Length & word count

| Script | Runtime | Spoken words | Pace | Avg sentence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper Billions — Spy | ~19:45 | ~3,240 | ~164 wpm | **7 words** |
| Paper Billions — Crash | ~15:00 | ~2,380 | ~159 wpm | 10 words |
| Stick Finance | ~7:15 | ~1,220 | ~169 wpm | 11 words |
| Martik Finance | ~8:20 | ~1,430 | ~172 wpm | 13 words |

- Average: ~12.5 min / ~2,070 words.
- **Target: 12–18 min, 2,000–3,000 words, ~160 wpm.**
- The favourite scripts have the *shortest* sentences. Short lines = one drawing beat each.

## 2. Opening hooks

| Script | Type | Line |
|---|---|---|
| PB Spy | Scene-drop + shocking reveal | "The boss of the most valuable company on Earth is having dinner with a Chinese spy." |
| PB Crash | Shocking number + mystery | "Nvidia lost more than $400 billion in a single morning, and it hadn't done anything wrong." |
| Stick | Relatable question | "Ever scrolled through social media and seen people talking about buying the dip?" |
| Martik | "Imagine you're…" hypothetical | "Imagine you're one of the richest people in the world…" |

**Winning formula (Paper Billions):** concrete shocking fact/scene (first 5 sec) → denial that deepens the mystery ("No bank failed. No law passed.") → a quick character-voice burst → the central question → teaser answer ("The short answer is…") → rewind ("The long answer starts…").

No channel intro or disclaimer interrupting the hook. (Martik's welcome + disclaimer at 0:52 is the weakest choice.)

## 3. Structure & pacing

- **Paper Billions:** 7–9 chapters of 1.5–3 min. Chapter titles are *story beats* ("The Locked Door", "The Empty Chair"), not topic labels. Arc: cold open → rewind → build to the cold-open moment → fallout → "what it means for you". Uses ticking clocks ("Nine thirty… Ten thirty-five…") and numbered lists ("Crack one… Crack four").
- **Stick Finance:** 9 chapters of 30–60 sec. Concept → analogy → worked example → takeaway. One recurring example character (Maya's sneaker shop) carries the middle.
- **Martik:** 12 chapters of ~40 sec. Concept → numeric example → caveat → real case (Archegos) → takeaways.
- **Universal rhythm:** a new analogy or example every 30–60 sec. Paper Billions adds a character-voice exchange every 20–40 sec.

## 4. Language & vocabulary

- ~6th–8th grade reading level. Jargon is defined the moment it appears ("The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge…").
- **Numbers translated into human terms:** "$600, about the price of a used couch"; "a new car, per chip"; "about $1,700 for every person in America"; "If a lemonade stand went from $33 to $3,000…"
- **Everyday analogies:** cake slices, concert VIP tickets, 10,000 line cooks flipping pancakes, renting cars parked over the border.
- **Character voices:** short dialogue from real people, imagined people, or personified things:
  - *"I'm diversified. Five hundred companies." / "Seven of them are a third of the pie."*
  - *"So how long have we got?" / "Thirty days." / "And if the new chip doesn't sell?" / "Twenty-nine."*
  - Jobs: deliver a punchline, stage a stat, or explain a mechanism as a two-line exchange.
- **Tics:** "Here's the strange part", "Hold that thought", "But here's the part almost nobody noticed", "Pretty cool, right?", "Ouch."
- **Callbacks:** a repeated line ("We make friends." ×4, "Dishes first") pays off at the end.

## 5. Transitions

1. Bridge sentence split across the chapter break ("So, where do people go shopping for these…" / "…a stock exchange.").
2. Question hand-offs ("You might be wondering…", "So who do you get angry at?").
3. Open loops ("Hold that thought." / "Nobody looks at those photos twice. Later, someone will.").
4. Time jumps ("Fast forward 5 years", "October, twenty twenty-two.").
5. Escalators ("But here's where things get even more interesting").

## 6. CTA placement

| Script | Placement |
|---|---|
| Stick | End: like + subscribe + "comment what topic next" |
| Martik | Intro + disclaimer at 0:52; like/subscribe at end |
| Paper Billions | **No spoken CTA** — ends on a callback or quote |

**Recommendation:** Paper Billions ending + one specific comment prompt. Optional soft mid-roll CTA at ~40%, at a natural pause, never over a reveal. Disclaimer as an on-screen note.

## 7. Audience

- ~18–40, curious non-experts, mostly US, holding index funds / 401(k)s / investing apps.
- Addressed directly: "If you're twenty-five, Thursday was a sale."
- Want: entertainment first, education second — *why did this happen* and *how does it hit my wallet*.
- Dislike: lectures, jargon, being talked down to.
- Stick/Martik = beginner "what is X" search traffic. Paper Billions = news-to-story, broader and more viral.

## 8. Whiteboard translation

| Script element | Drawing |
|---|---|
| Character-voice line | Stick figure / caricature + speech bubble; distinct VO voice |
| Human-scale number | Draw the comparison object next to the number |
| Ticking clock | A clock face that advances each scene |
| Numbered "cracks" | Literal cracks drawn across a wall / pie |
| Callback line | Reuse the exact drawing from first appearance |
| One idea per sentence | One draw stroke per sentence; 3–6 sec per scene |
