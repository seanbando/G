The $500 Stock Trap

[DRAW: Two price tags side by side: "$500" and "$5." A big question mark between them.]

One stock costs five hundred dollars. Another costs five dollars. Which company is bigger?

[PAUSE: 2 seconds. On screen: "Which company is bigger?"]

[DRAW: YOU, a stick figure holding a phone, pointing confidently at the $500 tag.]

YOU: The five-hundred-dollar one. Obviously.

[DRAW: A trap door opens under the $500 tag.]

That's the trap almost everyone falls for.

[DRAW: Pizza A cut into 3 slices, each labelled $500. Write "3 × $500 = $1,500."]

Picture each company as a pizza. Company A is cut into three slices at five hundred dollars each, so the whole pizza is worth fifteen hundred dollars.

[DRAW: Pizza B, drawn much bigger, cut into a huge number of tiny slices labelled $5. Write "1,000,000 × $5 = $5,000,000."]

Company B is cut into a million slices at five dollars each. That pizza is worth five million dollars.

[DRAW: Write "Share price × number of shares = market cap."]

Share price times the number of shares gives you market cap, and that's the number that tells you how big a company really is.

[DRAW: YOU staring at the giant Pizza B with wide eyes.]

YOU: So the cheap one was huge the whole time?

[DRAW: The narrator's stick figure tapping a note that says "How many slices?"]

Yep. Next time a stock looks cheap, check how many slices it's cut into.
