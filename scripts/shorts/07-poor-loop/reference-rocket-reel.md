# Reference: Instagram reel "why can't poor people become rich" (rocket / escape velocity)
Transcript supplied by the creator (we could not open the link: instagram.com is blocked in this environment).

## Structure
1. Hook question + myth bust: "Because they're lazy? No." -> paradox "being poor is expensive" (about 0:05)
2. Relatable setback list: car, rent, medical -> "boom, back down"
3. Metaphor pivot: "we have to understand rocket science" -> escape velocity, 99% falls back
4. Apply: work, save, emergency, progress gone
5. Mechanisms: bulk buying, cheap shoes (Vimes boots theory), skipped preventive care
6. Psychology: stress -> short-term decisions -> no assets -> low income -> loop
7. Ending: "work very, very hard... extraordinary things" -> escape velocity

## What works
- Myth bust in the first line (lazy? no) = instant disagreement/agreement hook
- One big metaphor that explains "close isn't enough" in a single image
- Named concept (boots theory) = feels like insider knowledge
- Loop stated as a chain the viewer can repeat

## Weak spots (our openings)
- Physics is wrong: at 99% of escape velocity a rocket climbs to about 50 Earth radii (~320,000 km, most of the way to the Moon at ~384,000 km); with 99% of the energy it would pass the Moon's distance. A nerd will correct it.
- No numbers at all; every mechanism is vague.
- Payoff is "work very, very hard", no tool, and it undercuts its own "not lazy" opener.
- Scarcity/bandwidth research is real (Mullainathan & Shafir) but some results failed to replicate; needs soft wording.

## Our version must
- Not reuse their lines (lazy opener, 99% line, shoe example) or beat order.
- Use real, sourced numbers (payday fees, overdraft, credit-based insurance).
- Make escape velocity a concrete, reachable thing (a buffer), not "work harder".
- Keep the physics right: "not enough speed and gravity pulls you back", no percent-of-the-way claim.
