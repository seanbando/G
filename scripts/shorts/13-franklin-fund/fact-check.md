# Fact-check: Ben Franklin's 200-Year Money Chest (Nov 20, 2026)

Structure: Money Mystery (guess hook -> ladder 1790 / 1890 / 1990 -> reveal -> question). Running image: the chest.
Single city (Boston) for simplicity; Philadelphia (about $2M in 1990) goes in the caption.

| Line | Claim | Source / math | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Money couldn't be fully used for 200 years | Codicil (June 23, 1789): spend part at 100 yrs, rest held to 200 yrs (Mental Floss, Britannica, Cambridge "Franklin and the Invention of Microfinance") | OK |
| 2 | £1,000 to Boston, about $4,500 | £1,000 each to Boston and Philadelphia; "about $4,500 in 1790s dollars" (search summaries). £1 ≈ $4.44 then -> $4,444 | OK, voice says "about" |
| 3 | Lent to young tradesmen at 5% | "lend at five per cent per annum to such young married artificers, under the age of twenty-five" | OK ("tradesmen" = artificers) |
| 4 | Franklin projected £131,000 at 100 yrs, £4,061,000 at 200 | Codicil projections (appstate "Ben's Life and Will", Philanthropy Roundtable) | OK |
| 5 | 1890 Boston fund $391,000 = two thirds of his math | $391,000 (appstate centennial page); £131,000 x 4.444 = $582,000; 391/582 = 0.67 | OK. Note: HistoryNet gives $431,756; $391,000 used (conservative) |
| 6 | Part built a trade school, rest reinvested | Franklin Union opened Sept 1908 (with Carnegie matching money) = today's Benjamin Franklin Institute of Technology | OK |
| 7-8 | 1990: almost $5 million; less than a third of his math | Reports range $4.5M-$5M (Ed Week 1990; BenefitsPro). £4,061,000 x 4.444 = $18.0M; 4.5-5.0 / 18.0 = 25-28% | OK ("less than a third") |
| 9 | Fewer borrowers, many didn't repay, money parked in safe accounts | Demand for loans "all but disappeared" mid-1800s; ~9 in 10 borrowers failed to repay (co-signers paid); managers moved capital into savings accounts / life insurance company (Cambridge book summaries, appstate, Syracuse thesis) | OK, "many" not "most" |

Caveats for the caption: nominal dollars, not inflation-adjusted; pound-to-dollar comparison uses the 1790s rate ($4.44/£). Philadelphia's fund reached about $2M by 1990 and funded scholarships.
Caption line: "Real story: Franklin's 1789 codicil. Figures are nominal, from city fund records as reported by historians; sources vary slightly."
