Ben Franklin's 200-Year Money Chest

He left a city a two-hundred-year bet. Guess how it ended.

Seventeen ninety-one. A thousand pounds, about forty-five hundred dollars, goes into the chest.

His rule: lend it to young tradesmen at five percent, and keep lending the interest.

He did the math himself. One hundred years, a hundred thirty-one thousand pounds. Two hundred years, over four million.

Eighteen ninety-one. The chest opens. Three hundred ninety-one thousand dollars. Only two thirds of his math.

The man who made the bet? Ben Franklin.

Part of it builds a trade school. The rest goes back in the chest.

Nineteen ninety-one. The chest opens again.

Almost five million dollars. Less than a third of what his math promised.

Fewer tradesmen qualified, many struggled to pay back, and the money didn't keep compounding like he planned.

The math never failed. People interrupted it.

So could you leave your money alone for twenty years? Yes or no.
