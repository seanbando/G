Ben Franklin's 200-Year Money Chest

He gave a city money that had to grow for two hundred years.

His name was Ben Franklin. Guess what it became.

Seventeen ninety. A thousand pounds, about forty-five hundred dollars, goes into the chest.

The rule: lend it to young tradesmen at five percent, and keep lending the interest.

He did the math himself. One hundred years, a hundred thirty-one thousand pounds. Two hundred years, over four million.

Eighteen ninety. The chest opens. Three hundred ninety-one thousand dollars. Only two thirds of his math.

Part of it builds a trade school. The rest goes back in the chest.

Nineteen ninety. The chest opens again.

Almost five million dollars. Less than a third of what his math promised.

Fewer tradesmen borrowed, many didn't pay back, and the money got parked in safe, slow accounts.

The math never failed. People interrupted it.

So could you leave your money alone for twenty years, let alone two hundred?
