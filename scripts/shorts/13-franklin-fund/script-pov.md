Ben Franklin's 200-Year Money Chest (POV: you are the money)

You're a thousand pounds. Locked in for two hundred years.

Ben Franklin's will sends you to Boston. Seventeen ninety-one: about forty-five hundred dollars.

Your job: get lent to young tradesmen at five percent, come back bigger, go out again.

His math: one hundred years, a hundred thirty-one thousand pounds. Two hundred years, over four million.

Some borrowers pay you back with interest. But fewer tradesmen qualify, many struggle to repay, and you don't get lent out enough.

Eighteen ninety-one. The chest opens. You're three hundred ninety-one thousand dollars. Only two thirds of his math.

Part of you builds a trade school. The rest goes back in the dark.

Nineteen ninety-one. Light again. You're almost five million dollars.

His math said almost four times that.

The math never failed. People interrupted you.

So could your money sit still for twenty years? Forget two hundred. Yes or no.
