# Fact-check: You're America's Last Penny (Nov 20, 2026)

Structure: POV ("you" are the last circulating penny). Ladder of eras with the penny shrinking, then the reveal (inflation, and your dollar), then a yes/no question.

| Line | Claim | Source / math | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | "America's last penny" | US Mint ceremonial strike of the final circulating one-cent coin, Philadelphia, Nov 12, 2025 (US Mint press release; AP/PBS). The Mint still makes limited collector pennies, so the caption says "last penny made for everyday spending" | OK with caption note |
| Hook | "cost almost four cents to make" | FY2024 unit cost 3.69 cents (US Mint annual report; Mint press release) | OK |
| 2 | Nov 12, 2025; Philadelphia; 232 years | Mint release: ends the penny's 232-year production run as a circulating coin. One outlet said Nov 11; Mint and AP say Nov 12 | OK |
| 3 | 3.69 cents each; about $85 million lost in 2024 | 3.69¢; about 3.17 billion pennies in FY2024 -> (3.69¢ - 1¢) x 3.17B = $85.3M net loss (CoinNews; CRS $117M gross cost) | OK ("about", net loss) |
| 5-7 | a 1913 cent buys what over 30¢ buys today; 1950 about 13¢; 1980 about 4¢ | BLS CPI-U annual averages: 1913 9.9, 1950 24.1, 1980 82.4, 2024 313.7; 2025 estimated at 2024 x 1.027 = 322.2. Ratios 32.5x, 13.4x, 3.9x | OK (approximate, "about"/"over") |
| 8 | stores round you away | Cash rounding to the nearest 5¢ is spreading (state guidance, Square, Atlanta Fed); not universal, card payments stay exact | OK as phrased |
| 10 | the dollar has lost about 97% of its buying power since 1913 | 1 - 9.9/322.2 = 96.9% | OK |
| Note | pennies still legal tender; about 250-300 billion still out there | Mint/AP (300B), American Bankers Association (250B) | caption only |

Caption disclaimer: "The final circulating penny was struck Nov 12, 2025 (the Mint still makes some collector pennies). Buying-power figures use the BLS CPI-U; 2025 is estimated. Pennies are still legal tender."

## Outside review (Nov 20, 2026), applied
- "Today: nothing" -> "Today: almost nothing" (a penny still has monetary value; still legal tender).
- "every year you bought less" -> "decade after decade you bought less" (some years had deflation, e.g. early 1920s, 1930s, 2009 for some months).
- "232 years of pennies end" -> "232 years of everyday pennies end" (circulating production ended; collector pennies continue).
- 1950: "about fourteen cents" in 2026 dollars (13.4 in 2025 dollars). 1913 "over thirty", 1980 "about four" and 97% still hold in 2026 dollars.
- Confirmed by reviewer: date, 3.69-cent cost, $85M loss, other comparisons, 97%.
