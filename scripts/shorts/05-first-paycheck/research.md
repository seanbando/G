# Research: You Just Got Your First Paycheck

Checked November 13, 2026. A worked example, not a real person.

## Example
Single filer, $20 an hour, 80 hours every two weeks (26 paychecks), standard W-4, no 401(k) or insurance deductions, living in a state with no state income tax (such as Texas or Florida).

| Item | Amount | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Gross pay | $1,600.00 | 20 x 80 |
| Social Security (6.2%) | $99.20 | 2026 employee rate 6.2% on wages up to $184,500 |
| Medicare (1.45%) | $23.20 | 1.45%, no wage cap |
| Federal income tax withheld | about $108 | Annual $41,600 minus 2026 single standard deduction $16,100 = $25,500 taxable; 10% of $12,400 + 12% of $13,100 = $2,812 a year, / 26 = $108.15. Actual withholding follows IRS Pub 15-T tables and lands close to this. |
| Take-home | about $1,369 | 1,600 - 99.20 - 23.20 - 108.15 = 1,369.45 |
| Taken | about $231 (14.4%) | |
| Employer's matching FICA | $122.40 | Employers pay another 6.2% + 1.45% |
| Total cost to the employer | $1,722.40 | |

## Facts
- **FICA:** employees pay 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 2026 wage base) and 1.45% Medicare (no cap); employers match both. (SSA figures via 1800accountant, ADP, Paychex)
- **2026 federal brackets, single:** 10% to $12,400, 12% to $50,400; standard deduction $16,100 (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 via NerdWallet, CNBC, NTU).
- **Withholding is an estimate:** if more is withheld than you owe for the year, the difference comes back as a refund; if less, you owe at tax time.
- **Social Security and Medicare are not personal savings accounts:** paying them earns work credits that make you eligible for benefits later. Never say the money is "saved for you".
- **States:** Texas and Florida have no state income tax; most states also withhold state income tax.
