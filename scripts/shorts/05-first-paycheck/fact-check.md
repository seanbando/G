# Fact check: You Just Got Your First Paycheck (revised 2026-11-13 after an outside review)

Example: single filer, $20/hour, 80 hours over two weeks (biweekly), standard W-4, no state or local income tax, no other deductions, 2026 rates. Federal withholding is set to an illustrative $108 (the annualised 2026 estimate is $108.15; actual withholding depends on the W-4, pay frequency and other details), so the on-screen numbers add up exactly.

| Claim | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 80 hours over two weeks at $20 = $1,600 gross | Verified | Arithmetic; "over two weeks" avoids overtime questions |
| Social Security 6.2% = $99.20 | Verified | 2026 employee rate, wages under the $184,500 base |
| Medicare 1.45% = $23.20 | Verified | No cap |
| Federal withholding $108 | Illustrative | Labelled on screen: "Illustrative example. Excludes state/local taxes and other deductions." |
| Taken $230.40; take-home $1,369.60 (spoken "about thirteen hundred seventy") | Verified | 99.20 + 23.20 + 108 = 230.40 |
| "Those first two slices fund Social Security and Medicare, and your work history helps decide what you qualify for" | Verified | Payroll taxes fund both programs; work credits are part of eligibility (Medicare is health coverage, with other requirements too) |
| "The income tax slice is just an estimate... you get the extra back after you file" | Verified | Applies to income tax withholding only, not Social Security or Medicare |
| Employer pays another $122.40 | Verified | Employer matches 6.2% + 1.45% |
| $1,722.40 | Verified, labelled | "Employer cost: wages + employer payroll taxes"; excludes other benefits and other employer taxes; not take-home pay |
