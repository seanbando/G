# Fact-check: The Million-Dollar Button (Nov 22, 2026)

Structure: POV dilemma (button), fake-out ("free money"), escalation, twist (your money shrinks too), named payoff (inflation), real-world proof, value, question. Metaphor: a shrink ray on every dollar (new money doesn't take anyone's dollars; it lowers what each dollar buys), chosen over a wrecking ball because inflation doesn't destroy money. No sentence starts with a year or number.

| Claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| More money chasing the same goods pushes prices up | Standard explanation (simplified: inflation happens when money/spending grows faster than the supply of goods) | textbook |
| US money supply (M2) grew about 40% between 2020 and 2022 | OK: about $15.5T (Feb 2020) -> $21.7T (Feb 2022), about +40% | St. Louis Fed, "The Rise and Fall of M2"; S&P Global (peak $21.74T, Mar 2022) |
| Then prices rose the fastest in 40 years | OK: CPI-U 12-month change peaked at 9.1% in June 2022, the highest since 1981 | BLS; S&P Global ("highest level in 40 years") |
| "Not the only cause, but a big one" | Hedge required: supply chains, energy and demand shifts also drove 2021-22 inflation; economists debate the weights | St. Louis Fed (inflation followed M2 growth with a lag) |
| Cash that sits still loses buying power when prices rise | OK by definition | |
| "people try to own things that outgrow prices" | General education, not a recommendation | |
