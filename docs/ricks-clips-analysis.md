# Ricks Clips Analysis (@RicksClips3000)

Built on November 13, 2026 from vidIQ data pulled on November 12 (his 50 most popular shorts with views, lengths and comments) and three full transcripts (Jar, Railroad, Japan job). YouTube and vidIQ were unavailable for more pulls at the time.

## Format switch

| Period | Format | Length | Typical views |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apr to Jul 2026 | Meme clips with emoji titles | 7 to 31 s | 2 to 7M |
| Oct to Nov 2026 | Narrated stories | 40 to 66 s | 9 to 33M |

Top three are all 61 to 66 s (Jar 33.6M, Railroads 28.3M, Japan job 17.7M), but a 40 s story still did 15.7M. The story drives it, not the length.

No emoji or hashtags on story titles; 4 to 8 words. Money and gambling stories already work for him: Gambling Window 15.4M, Second Draw 9.5M, Cashiers Lottery 8.8M, Winning Tickets 6.4M, Mirror Horse Race 5.5M.

## Script anatomy

About 220 words in about 62 s (3.4 to 3.7 words a second), 10 to 13 words a sentence. Seven beats every time:

1. **Strange act plus stake (0 to 3 s):** "He put a bronze ball in a dragon's mouth and told the emperor, 'If this ever falls out, run.'" / "He saw gaps into a railroad every 39 ft." / "It's impossible to get fired in Japan, but companies found something way worse."
2. **"You see," setup (3 to 10 s):** the world and the stakes.
3. **Doubters with dialogue (10 to 20 s):** "The whole court burst out laughing." / "'You're wrecking my railroad.'"
4. **The fake-out (20 to 35 s):** the story seems to prove the doubters right. "A ball fell, but nothing happened." / "His gaps start shrinking... 'Even your gaps know you were wrong.'" / "At first, it's paradise." This second open question carries viewers through the middle.
5. **Time-jump escalation (30 to 50 s):** "Weeks later... Then one night..." / "In May... By July..."
6. **Disaster proves the hero right (45 to 55 s):** "The next morning, the palace was a pile of rubble."
7. **Mechanism last (52 to 62 s):** the hidden pendulum explained; or a cut mid-sentence so it loops; or a one-line truth ("because nobody can do nothing forever").

## Language rules

- Characters have no names ("he", "the emperor", "his boss").
- Concrete, physical details and sound words ("clicketity clack").
- Short dialogue lines, usually from the doubters.
- The concept is shown, never named.
- Present tense or "you" to put the viewer inside the story.
- Few numbers, small and specific.
- No call to action, ever.

## What to take for CashleyFinance

1. Put a fake-out in the middle of every script: a beat where it looks like the opposite is true.
2. Show the concept before naming it; name it only after the viewer has seen it happen.
3. Alternate: news shorts (Afterhour style, famous names, short life) for spikes, and Ricks-style evergreen money stories (scams, gambling and lottery mechanisms, market history) that keep getting views for months.
